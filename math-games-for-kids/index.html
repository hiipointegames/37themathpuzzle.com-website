Math games for kids, students and classrooms

The math games children keep opening are free, take a few minutes rather than a class period, and feel like a puzzle rather than a test. 37 - The Math Puzzle gives students seven dice and a target of 37, which is order-of-operations practice in disguise, and it is self-checking so they correct themselves instead of waiting to be marked.

Why worksheets lose and puzzles win

The usual reason a child rejects math practice is that it feels like being tested on something they already got wrong. A puzzle with more than one correct answer removes that: there is no single response to be marked down on, and the dice roll makes each session feel like a fresh problem rather than another page of the same one.

By grade level

5th grade

Parentheses and the order of operations are introduced in 5th grade under US Common Core standard 5.OA.A.1, published in 2010 and still current as of August 2026. The maths is within reach, and Easy is the right setting — seven white dice and no Power Dice rules, with a Practice version that has no clock. Because of the 13+ store rating, at this age it works as a class or family activity on a shared screen rather than on a pupil's own device.

6th grade

The sweet spot. Whole-number exponents arrive in 6th grade under standard 6.EE.A.1, which is the point where all three Power Dice rules become playable. A 6th grader has every tool the full Daily Challenge asks for.

7th grade and up

By 7th grade most students are fluent, so the question shifts from whether they can solve it to how fast, and whether they can land a Perfect Solve using all three Power Dice. Streaks, the daily podium and Blitz Colosseum give that age group something to compete over.

For teachers

Teachers usually screen for three things at once: genuinely free, no student accounts to administer, and something that runs on whatever hardware the school owns. 37 - The Math Puzzle is free with no subscription or paywall, and the browser version runs on a Chromebook, a school laptop or an interactive whiteboard with no download and no app store approval needed.

Because the Daily Challenge deals every player the same seven dice, a whole class works the identical problem and can compare different routes to 37. That makes it a five-minute warm-up or bell-ringer. Easy strips out the Power Dice rules, and Practice removes the clock, for students who need a gentler entry.

It also travels over the summer: a new puzzle appears every day whether or not school is in session, so it works as a low-effort summer assignment or a voluntary class streak.

For homeschool families

The same shared-roll mechanic means siblings at different levels can attempt one puzzle together, with a parent handling the trickier operations for a younger child.

A note on the age rating

37 - The Math Puzzle is rated 13+ on both the App Store and Google Play. There is nothing objectionable in the content — it is dice and arithmetic — but the game is ad-supported, shows player-chosen usernames on public leaderboards, and is built for a general teen and adult audience rather than as a children's product.

In practice that means: students aged 13 and over can play on their own devices, and younger pupils are best served by a teacher-led session on a shared screen or an interactive whiteboard, or by playing alongside a parent at home. The browser version at 37themathpuzzle.app is well suited to exactly that, since one adult-run screen serves a whole class.
