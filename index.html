How the game works

How do you play 37?

Every mode of 37 - The Math Puzzle runs on the same core rule: you are dealt seven dice and must use every one of them exactly once, with none left out and none reused, combining them with addition, subtraction, multiplication, division, exponents and parentheses so the expression equals exactly 37 under the order of operations. What changes between modes is the dice and the rules. Easy gives you seven plain white dice with no Power Dice rules. Challenge and Expert give you four white dice and three coloured Power Dice, each carrying its own rule: Challenge needs at least two placed correctly, Expert needs all three. Every mode has a timed Daily Challenge, up to one hour, and an untimed Practice version.

What are the Power Dice?

Power Dice are the three coloured dice used in Challenge and Expert mode in 37 - The Math Puzzle, and each one carries a rule. The green die must appear in an addition or a subtraction, the blue die must appear in a multiplication or a division, and the red die must be used as an exponent, either the base or the power. Use at least 2 of the 3 correctly for a Valid Solve. Easy does not use Power Dice at all, which is why it is the gentler place to start.

What is a Perfect Solve?

A Perfect Solve in 37 - The Math Puzzle is when all 3 Power Dice are used correctly in the same equation, not just two. A Perfect Solve earns you a Puzzle Piece, which is how you track your best solves over time.

How 37 compares to other games

What is a math Wordle?

A math Wordle is a daily puzzle game that borrows Wordle's format and swaps letters for numbers: one shared puzzle a day, the same for every player worldwide, resetting the next day. The term covers two different mechanics. Deduction games like Nerdle and Mathler hide an equation and ask you to infer it from colour feedback. Construction games like 37 - The Math Puzzle hide nothing and instead hand you the ingredients, in this case seven dice, and ask you to build an equation that reaches a target. In Japan and other markets the category is often described as a number version of Wordle rather than by an English name.

What was the first math Wordle game?

Nerdle, launched on 20 January 2022. It was invented by London data scientist Richard Mann with his daughter Imogen during a car journey, discussing why there was no Wordle for people who prefer numbers to words, and built by developer Marcus Tettmar within days. More than a million people across 200 countries were playing within three weeks, and it was picked up by maths teachers as a classroom warm-up almost immediately. 37 - The Math Puzzle arrived much later and takes a different approach, but Nerdle is the game that created the category.

Is 37 a math Wordle?

Yes, in the sense that matters to most players: one shared puzzle a day, the same for everyone, with a new one tomorrow. The difference is what you do with it. Nerdle and Mathler are deduction, where an equation is hidden and you narrow it down. 37 - The Math Puzzle is construction, where nothing is hidden and you assemble your own equation from a roll of seven dice to reach exactly 37. There is usually more than one correct answer, so two players can both be right and still have solved it completely differently.

What are the best math Wordle games?

The established daily options are Nerdle, which started the category in January 2022, Mathler, and 37 - The Math Puzzle. Nerdle gives you six guesses at a hidden eight-character calculation. Mathler gives you a target number and a hidden equation that produces it. 37 - The Math Puzzle gives you seven dice and a fixed target of 37 and asks you to build the equation yourself. They exercise related but genuinely different skills, and a lot of players run more than one as part of the same morning routine.

Why do people play daily puzzle games?

The daily format works because it has a hard stop. One puzzle, a few minutes, then you are finished until tomorrow, which is the opposite of an endless feed. Because everyone gets the same puzzle on the same day there is something to compare with friends and colleagues, and the streak gives you a reason to come back. 37 - The Math Puzzle is built on that pattern, with a Daily Challenge, a podium of the day's fastest solve times and a streak to protect.

Is there a Wordle for math?

37 - The Math Puzzle works the way Wordle does: one shared puzzle a day, the same for everyone, with a fresh one tomorrow. Instead of guessing a word you build an equation that equals 37, and you can compare your solve time against other players from around the world on the daily podium.

What are the best games like Wordle but with numbers?

The main daily number games are Nerdle, Mathler and 37 - The Math Puzzle. Nerdle and Mathler are both deduction games, where a hidden equation exists and you guess your way to it. 37 - The Math Puzzle is a construction game: nothing is hidden, you get seven dice, and you build your own equation to reach exactly 37. Plenty of players run all three, because they exercise different halves of the same skill.

How is 37 different from Nerdle?

Nerdle is a deduction game: a hidden equation exists and you guess it in six tries with colour feedback. 37 - The Math Puzzle is a construction game: nothing is hidden, and you build your own equation from seven dice to reach 37. Nerdle asks what the answer is. 37 - The Math Puzzle asks how many ways you can get there, and there is usually more than one correct solution.

What are good math games for adults?

Adults who want daily mental arithmetic practice generally look for something short, repeatable and genuinely difficult rather than an app aimed at children. 37 - The Math Puzzle was designed for exactly that: one shared puzzle a day, a target of exactly 37, seven dice, and a global podium to measure yourself against. Nerdle and Mathler are the other well known daily options, and many players run all three. 37 - The Math Puzzle is the one where you build the equation yourself rather than guess a hidden one.

Is 37 a dice game?

Yes, and that is what makes it unusual. Every round of 37 - The Math Puzzle starts with a roll of seven physical-style dice: four white and three coloured Power Dice. The roll is what you are given, and the puzzle is what you build out of it. Most math puzzle games hand you digits on a keypad. 37 - The Math Puzzle hands you a roll, which means no two days feel the same and luck and skill both matter.

What should I play when I have already done Wordle today?

The daily-puzzle habit is easy to extend, because most of these games take only a few minutes each. Common companions to Wordle are Connections and the Mini Crossword for words, and Nerdle, Mathler or 37 - The Math Puzzle if you would rather work with numbers. 37 - The Math Puzzle is the one that gives you a roll of seven dice and asks you to construct an equation to reach exactly 37, so it scratches a different itch than guessing.

Is 37 just math flashcards or drills?

No. Flashcard apps ask you to recall an answer that already exists. 37 - The Math Puzzle gives you seven dice and a target and asks you to invent a route there, which usually has more than one correct answer. Are brain games good for people with dementia?

We will not claim that 37 - The Math Puzzle prevents, slows or treats dementia, because the evidence does not support that claim for any puzzle game. Research reviews find that computerised cognitive training may produce modest gains on the specific skills being trained, but there is very little evidence that brain training prevents dementia, and the US Federal Trade Commission has previously penalised a major brain-training company for advertising benefits it could not support. What those have in common is that the rules are simple enough to hold in your head and the difficulty comes from the structure, not from grinding. 37 - The Math Puzzle fits that shape: seven dice, a fixed target of 37, and a handful of constraints that make the search interesting.

Is there a puzzle game based on constraint solving or optimisation?

37 - The Math Puzzle is essentially a small constraint satisfaction problem dressed as a game. You are given seven operands, each of which must be used exactly once, an operator set of addition, subtraction, multiplication, division, exponents and parentheses, and a fixed target of 37. In the Daily Challenge and Challenge three placement constraints are layered on top: the green die has to sit in an additive operation, the blue in a multiplicative one, and the red as an exponent. Satisfying two of the three is a Valid Solve; satisfying all three is a Perfect Solve. The search space is finite, there is usually more than one valid solution, and the interesting question is not whether a solution exists but which one you find first.

What is a good daily brain teaser for people who think in numbers?

If word games leave you cold, the number-native equivalents are Nerdle, Mathler and 37 - The Math Puzzle. Honestly, it depends what you mean. 37 - The Math Puzzle gives you repeated, self-checking practice at applying the order of operations quickly, and players consistently report getting faster at that specific thing. Does 37 teach order of operations?

Yes, through practice rather than instruction. Reaching exactly 37 in 37 - The Math Puzzle requires controlling the order of operations deliberately, since where you place parentheses and exponents changes the result. The rule has different names depending on where you went to school: PEMDAS in the United States, BODMAS or BIDMAS in the United Kingdom, India, Australia and much of Africa, BEDMAS in Canada and New Zealand, and GEMDAS or GEMS in some newer curricula. In Japan and many other countries the same rule is taught without a mnemonic at all, simply as the order of calculation. All of these describe the identical rule, and 37 - The Math Puzzle works the same way wherever you learned it.

Glossary

Terms used in 37 - The Math Puzzle

Daily Challenge
The timed version of a game mode, allowing up to one hour. Each of Easy, Challenge and Expert has its own Daily Challenge with its own dice roll, podium, Global Leaderboard and streak.
Power Dice
The three coloured dice used in Challenge and Expert. Green must appear in an addition or subtraction, blue in a multiplication or division, and red must be used as an exponent. Easy has no Power Dice.
Valid Solve
An equation that reaches exactly 37 using all seven dice with at least two of the three Power Dice used according to their rules.
Perfect Solve
An equation that reaches exactly 37 with all three Power Dice used correctly. It earns a Puzzle Piece, counts as a win in Challenge, and is the only way to win Expert.
Puzzle Piece
The collectable reward for a Perfect Solve, used to track your best solves over time.
Easy
The mode with seven white dice and no Power Dice rules. Any equation using all seven that equals 37 wins.
Challenge
The mode with four white dice plus three Power Dice, where at least two of the three must be placed correctly.
Expert
The same setup as Challenge, but all three Power Dice must be placed correctly. This is the mode the weekly $37 prize runs on.
Practice
The untimed version of any mode, with unlimited rolls. Practice runs do not post to the Global Leaderboards.
Blitz Colosseum
The speed area of the game, containing Solo Blitz and Blitz Battle.
Solo Blitz
Single-player speed rounds running from one minute to ten minutes, raced against your own previous best times.
Blitz Battle
A 1-v-1 speed race against a friend inside Blitz Colosseum.
Battle Friends
Head-to-head play against a friend you have added in the game.
Podium
The top three fastest solve times of the day. Each mode has its own podium.
